You classify how one Czech court decision treats another decision it cites.

## Metadata

- Citing decision: {{citing_court}}, panel type `{{citing_panel}}`, decided {{citing_date}}
- Cited decision: {{cited_court}}, decided {{cited_date}}

## What the cited decision held

{{cited_ratio}}

## Context from the citing decision

The citation appears in the middle paragraph. The surrounding paragraphs are given for context only.

```
{{context}}
```

## Labels

Choose exactly one.

| Label | Use when |
|---|---|
| `FOLLOWED` | The citing court applies the cited rule and agrees with it. |
| `MENTIONED` | Background reference only. The court does not rely on it. Includes citations that merely recount what a party argued. |
| `DISTINGUISHED` | The court accepts the cited rule but holds that the facts before it differ, so the rule does not apply here. |
| `NARROWED` | The court accepts the cited rule but limits the range of situations it governs. The rule survives in reduced form. |
| `DEPARTED` | The court replaces the cited interpretation with a different one. The old reading no longer stands as this court states the law. |
| `CRITICIZED` | The court disagrees with the cited reasoning but does not have, or does not exercise, the authority to change it. |
| `QUASHED` | The citing decision formally annuls the cited decision in its operative part (výrok). |

The three that are easy to confuse:

- `DISTINGUISHED` leaves the rule untouched and says "not these facts".
- `NARROWED` changes the rule's scope and says "the rule holds, but only for ...".
- `DEPARTED` changes the rule's content and says "the earlier reading was wrong".

Do not infer `DEPARTED` from the panel type alone. A rozšířený senát, velký senát or plénum
decision is often asked to depart and then declines to. Read what the text actually says.

## Output

Return one JSON object and nothing else.

```json
{
  "label": "NARROWED",
  "confidence": 0.82,
  "evidence_span": "verbatim substring of the context above",
  "reasoning": "one sentence"
}
```

Rules on `evidence_span`, which are checked mechanically and are not negotiable:

1. It must be copied character for character out of the **Context** block above. Not out of
   the metadata, not out of the cited decision's holding, not paraphrased, not translated.
2. It must be the passage that actually carries your label. If no such passage exists in the
   context, the honest label is `MENTIONED` with low confidence.
3. Keep it to one or two sentences.

`confidence` is your probability that the label is right, in [0, 1]. Be conservative on
`DEPARTED`: a wrong `DEPARTED` produces a red light for the user, and a false red is far
more costly than a false amber.
